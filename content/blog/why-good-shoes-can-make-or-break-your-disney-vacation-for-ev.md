At Disney World, we walk a lot. A normal park day can mean miles of walking, standing in lines, hopping on and off attractions, and being on your feet from morning until fireworks.

And if your feet hurt? It can change the entire day.

Over the years, we've learned something important: there really isn't one magical pair of "Disney shoes" that's perfect for everybody.

In fact, our own family doesn't even agree on the best shoes. 😂

Some of us love supportive athletic shoes. One of us would happily live in Crocs. Seth has his own favorite shoes and doesn't want anything to do with ours.

That's exactly why our biggest advice is this:

The best Disney shoe isn't the shoe somebody on the internet tells you to buy. It's the shoe that works for your feet—and that you've actually tested before your trip.

## 1. Comfort Comes Before Cute

I love putting together Disney outfits as much as anyone, but when we're talking about shoes, comfort wins. Every time.

Disney vacations can involve long days on hard pavement, and shoes that feel perfectly comfortable during a quick trip to the store may feel completely different after hours in the parks.

Look for shoes that fit your feet well, give you the support you need, and don't rub or create pressure points.

And don't assume the shoe everyone else is raving about will automatically work for you.

This is definitely not a one-shoe-fits-all situation.

Seth is actually a perfect example of why we don't believe in declaring one particular shoe "the best."

## 2. What Our Family Actually Wears at Disney

Our family's shoe choices are all over the place—and I think that's a good thing.

Seth loves his [Hey Dudes](accessories-footwear.html). That's what he is comfortable wearing, so that's what works for him.

Arianna lives in [Crocs](accessories-footwear.html). Seriously. 😂 That's what she likes. Tennis shoes actually hurt her feet horribly, so for her, the shoe that many people automatically think of as the "right" choice for a long day of walking is anything but.

And she isn't the only one in our family. My mom can't comfortably wear tennis shoes either, and my daughter-in-law learned during one of our Disney trips that the tennis shoes she'd chosen weren't nearly as comfortable for a full park day as she'd expected.

Troy and I rotate between [On](accessories-footwear.html) and [Hoka](accessories-footwear.html). We own several pairs because those are the brands we've found comfortable for our own feet during long park days.

![Close-up of a white Hoka shoe being worn during a Disney park day.](photos/blog-shoes-hoka-closeup.jpg)

That's exactly why I hesitate to tell anyone that there's one "best" shoe for Disney. Even within our own family, what feels amazing on one person's feet can be miserable on someone else's.

The goal isn't to wear the shoe you're supposed to wear to Disney.

It's to figure out what works for your feet before you get there.

## 3. Break Them In Before Your Vacation

Please don't buy brand-new shoes, pack them in your suitcase, and let your first full day at Magic Kingdom be their test run. 😬

Even a great shoe can rub differently than expected once you've been walking for hours.

Wear your Disney shoes ahead of time. Take walks in them. Run errands in them. Spend several hours on your feet.

If something pinches, rubs or starts creating a hot spot at home, you definitely don't want to discover that several miles into a park day.

And if you're planning to rotate between two pairs at Disney, test both pairs before you leave.

## 4. Kids' Shoes Matter Too

The same rule applies to kids: don't make Disney the testing ground for brand-new footwear.

Choose something comfortable, secure and appropriate for a long, active park day—and make sure they've already spent time walking in it.

Cute Disney-themed shoes can be fun, but if they're uncomfortable after two hours, nobody is going to care how adorable they looked that morning. 😂

For younger children especially, I prefer secure shoes that stay on their feet and protect their toes while they're walking through crowds, climbing in and out of rides and navigating a very busy park.

## 5. Don't Forget the Socks

A great pair of shoes can only do so much if your socks are working against you.

For long Disney days, we prefer comfortable socks that fit properly and don't bunch, slide around or create friction.

Florida also has a special talent for combining heat + humidity + surprise rainstorm into the same afternoon. 😂 If your feet tend to get sweaty or wet, moisture-managing socks can make a noticeable difference.

I also think packing an extra pair of socks in the park bag is one of those tiny things that can save the day after a downpour or an unexpectedly soaked pair of shoes.

## 6. Have a Blister Plan

Even when you've done everything right, feet can still get irritated.

We like having [blister bandages or other blister protection](sun-travel-personal-care.html) in our park bag so we can deal with a hot spot when we first notice it, rather than waiting until it becomes painful.

That's one of those little Disney lessons we've learned over time:

Don't wait until your feet are miserable to do something about them.

Protect the spot, make an adjustment, change your socks if necessary, and keep enjoying your day.

## So…What Shoes Should You Wear to Disney?

The ones that work for you.

Not mine.

Not Troy's.

Not Seth's.

Not Arianna's.

And definitely not a pair you bought because somebody on TikTok said they're the only shoes you should ever wear to Disney. 😂

Start with shoes you already know your feet like. If you're buying something new, give yourself plenty of time to test them before vacation.

Our family happens to wear Hey Dudes, Crocs, On and Hoka, and you can find the styles we personally use in our [Shop My Finds / Pixie Family Picks](accessories-footwear.html) if you'd like a starting point.

But consider those ideas—not rules.

Because the goal isn't finding the internet's "best Disney shoe."

It's finding the pair that lets you stop thinking about your feet and get back to enjoying Disney with your family.
