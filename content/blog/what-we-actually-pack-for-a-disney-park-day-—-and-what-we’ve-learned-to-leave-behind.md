Real-life Disney packing tips from a family that has learned there’s no such thing as one perfect park bag.

There was a time when I thought being prepared for a Disney park day meant packing for every possible situation.

Extra this. Backup that. Something for the heat. Something for the rain. Snacks. Drinks. Chargers. Fans. Ponchos.

And somehow, by the time we reached the park, it felt like we were carrying enough supplies to establish a small settlement in Frontierland. 😂

After years of Walt Disney World trips, though, our family has gotten much better at figuring out what we actually need.

And one of the biggest things we've learned?

The perfect Disney park bag doesn't exist.

What we carry changes depending on the weather, how long we're staying, which park we're visiting and—most importantly—who is with us that day.

When Seth is with us, our setup looks very different because we need to make sure he has everything necessary to stay comfortable throughout the day. When Troy and I head into a park without him, I may carry nothing more than a belt bag while Troy carries a backpack.

So instead of giving you another giant list of things you must carry into Disney World, here's what years of Disney park days have taught our family about packing smarter.

1. Start With the People — Not the Packing List

Before deciding what goes in your park bag, think about the people going with you.

A family spending twelve hours at Magic Kingdom with small children is going to need very different things than a couple heading into EPCOT for dinner.

And our own packing changes dramatically depending on whether Seth is with us.

Because of Seth's needs, we bring additional items to keep him comfortable throughout the day. His wheelchair also gives us places to attach and carry some of those things—which is incredibly helpful—but it means our setup can start looking like a traveling Disney supply station pretty quickly. 😂

We usually have drinks, snacks, fans, cooling items and other things we know he'll need available throughout the day.

On other trips or park days, we can go much lighter.

That's why my first Disney park-bag rule is simple:

Don't pack for somebody else's family. Pack for yours.

2. Florida Heat Changes Everything

If you're visiting Walt Disney World during the hotter months, staying cool isn't just something we think about after we get uncomfortable.

It's part of how we pack.

Portable fans are probably some of the hardest-working items in our Disney gear. We use handheld fans, and we've also found ways to attach fans to Seth's wheelchair so he can have airflow while we're moving through the parks.

And then there are the cooling towels.

We use them.

We love them.

And apparently Seth has decided that wearing one around his neck is far too conventional. 😂

He often wears his cooling towel on his head underneath his hat, which may not be appearing on a Disney fashion runway anytime soon, but it works!

I've worn mine around my neck plenty of times, too.

That's something we've learned after enough hot Disney days: when everyone starts getting overheated, nobody cares how Instagram-worthy the cooling strategy is.

Comfort wins.

![](/photos/pasted-image-1787533774939.png)

3. Sometimes the Mouse Ears Stay Home

I love my Disney ears.

Obviously. 😂

But there are days when Florida makes the decision for me.

On especially hot or sunny park days, I'll sometimes leave my ears at home and wear a lightweight hat instead. It gives me actual sun coverage and can be much more comfortable after several hours outside.

That's one of those little things I don't think about much anymore because we've been doing Disney for so long.

But it's also exactly the kind of thing I wish someone had told me before some of our earlier trips.

You don't have to look perfectly Disney-styled every minute of your vacation.

Sometimes the smartest Disney accessory is the one keeping the sun off your face.

Cute ears or Florida survival?

Florida survival occasionally wins. 😂

![](/photos/pasted-image-1787533730330.png)

4. Bring Your Own Drinks and Snacks

We almost always have drinks with us, and when Seth is along, having familiar snacks readily available can make the day much easier.

It also means we aren't constantly stopping because someone suddenly needs something.

That's an important distinction:

We're not packing snacks because we're trying to carry an entire pantry through Disney World.

We're packing strategically.

Bring the things your family will realistically eat and drink.

Especially if someone in your group has particular preferences, needs familiar foods, or simply gets very unhappy when they're hungry. And yes, that last category may include adults. 😂

The goal is to make the park day easier—not make your bag heavier just because a packing list told you to bring something.

5. Your Park Bag Doesn't Have to Be a Backpack

This might be one of the biggest changes I've made over the years.

Sometimes I don't need a traditional park bag at all.

If we're heading into Disney without Seth and don't need all of our additional gear, I may wear a small belt bag while Troy carries a backpack.

That's it.

On those days, we're not carrying things “just in case.” We're bringing what we're reasonably likely to use.

And it is wonderful.

A Loungefly-sized backpack can also be a great middle ground when you need more than a belt bag but don't want a large backpack.

Think about the kind of day you're planning.

A few hours at EPCOT?

You probably don't need the same bag you'd pack for rope drop through fireworks at Magic Kingdom.

6. Prepare for Rain — Without Packing for a Hurricane

If you've spent much time in Florida, you already know where this is going.

Blue sky.

Sunshine.

Everything is beautiful.

And then...

RAIN. 😂

We like having rain protection available, but again, what we bring depends on the forecast and the day.

Ponchos are easy to pack and don't take up much space. An umbrella can provide rain protection and bonus shade when the sun returns—which it probably will approximately seventeen minutes later. 😂

The important part is having something.

Because buying emergency rain gear after the sky opens is a lot less fun than pulling something out of your bag and continuing your day.

7. Make Your Gear Work Harder

One thing we've learned from traveling with Seth is how valuable it is when something can serve more than one purpose—or when we can make our existing setup work better.

We attach fans where they're useful.

We use the wheelchair to organize drinks and supplies.

We keep things we need frequently within reach instead of buried at the bottom of a bag.

And we've learned where everything goes so we aren't unloading half our belongings every time somebody needs sunscreen.

You don't need a wheelchair setup to use the same idea.

Whether you have a stroller, backpack, belt bag or Loungefly, think about access, not just capacity.

The biggest bag isn't automatically the best bag.

Sometimes a smaller, well-organized setup is much easier to live with for eight hours.

8. Don't Pack Your Fears

This might be the biggest lesson we've learned.

Disney packing lists can get enormous because they're usually built around one thought:

“But what if I need it?”

What if somebody gets cold?

What if somebody gets hot?

What if it rains?

What if my phone dies?

What if someone wants a snack?

What if we need seventeen Band-Aids and an emergency sewing kit somewhere between Pirates of the Caribbean and Haunted Mansion?

You can “what if” yourself into carrying a 30-pound backpack. 😂

There are certain things we're absolutely willing to carry because experience has taught us that our family uses them.

But we've also gotten comfortable leaving things behind.

Disney World isn't the wilderness.

If we unexpectedly need something, chances are we're going to be able to figure it out.

I'd rather solve one unusual problem occasionally than carry the solution to fifty imaginary problems on every park day.

What Usually Makes Our Disney Park-Day Cut

Our exact setup changes, but these are the kinds of things that consistently earn space:

A comfortable park bag, backpack or belt bag appropriate for that particular day

Refillable drinks/water

A few snacks we know we'll actually eat

Portable fans

Cooling towels during hot weather

Hats/sun protection

Sunscreen and SPF lip balm

Rain protection when the forecast calls for it

Portable phone charger

Any personal, medical or accessibility-related items our family needs

Notice what's missing?

Everything else. 😂

You can always add what your family needs.

That's the whole point.

Pack for Your Family, Not the Internet

If I could go back and give my earlier Disney-planning self one piece of advice, it would be this:

You don't have to prepare for every possible Disney scenario.

Prepare for your people.

Think about the weather.

Think about how long you'll actually be in the park.

Think about what you've used on previous outings.

Think about what makes someone in your family more comfortable.

And then give yourself permission to leave the rest behind.

Some Disney days, we're rolling into the park with Seth's chair loaded with fans, drinks, cooling gear, snacks and everything else we know he'll need.

Other days, I'm wearing a belt bag and we're off.

Both are the right way to pack.

Because the goal isn't having the perfect Disney park bag.

The goal is spending less time worrying about everything you brought—and more time enjoying the people you brought with you.
